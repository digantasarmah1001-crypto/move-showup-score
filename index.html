    Five people invited you tonight. Who do you pick?

    On MOVE you join a plan with someone new, the same day. Afterwards both people rate each other from 1 to 5★, and the app knows who actually showed up.

    Each row is one person. Each dot is one of their plans in the last 90 days:

    showed up cancelled late no-show

    MOVE needs one number that says “this person will show up”. Let's try three ways to calculate it.

    Model A

    Average rating

    The obvious score: add up someone's ratings and divide by how many there are.

    sum of ratings ÷ number of ratings

    Result

    Sam and Nico tie for first place with a perfect 5.00.

    The flaw

    • Sam has a single rating. One lucky review beats Marta's nineteen.
    • Nico missed 15 of his 20 plans. Nobody can rate you if you don't show up, so A never sees it. His missed plans are faded because A ignores them.

    Model B · fixes “one lucky review”

    Bayesian average

    Everyone starts with 5 pretend ratings of 3.5★, then their real ratings are added. A few reviews can't move you far from 3.5; many reviews can.

    (5 × 3.5★ + ratings) ÷ (5 + number of ratings)

    What changed

    • ▼4Sam 1st → 5th. One 5★ against five pretend 3.5★ ratings gives 3.75.
    • ▲1Nico 2nd → 1st. Five real 5★ ratings outweigh the pretend ones: 4.25.

    The flaw

    • B still only reads ratings. Nico's 15 no-shows are invisible, and now he's in first place.

    Model C · fixes “no-shows are invisible”

    Show-up score

    Every plan counts: showed up = 1, cancelled late = ½, no-show = 0. Recent plans count more, so a plan from 30 days ago counts half. Then 70% showing up is mixed with 30% rating.

    100 × (70% show-up rate + 30% rating)

    What changed

    • ▼4Nico 1st → 5th, score 44. His no-shows count now, and his last 10 plans were all no-shows.
    • ▲1Ana 3rd → 2nd, score 80. Flaky two months ago, reliable since. Old plans weigh less, so she recovers.
    • ▲2Sam 5th → 3rd, score 73. One plan says little either way, so a new user starts in the middle.

    The trade-off

    • How much should the past count? How much should ratings count? These are choices, not facts. Next, change them yourself.
